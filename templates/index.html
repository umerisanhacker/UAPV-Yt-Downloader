Frequently Asked Questions

Why are some formats split into audio and video? ▼
YouTube uses Adaptive Bitrate Streaming (DASH) for resolutions 1080p and higher, meaning they store the video and audio tracks separately. Our engine downloads the highest quality version of both and seamlessly merges them together using FFmpeg.
What is the difference between AV1, VP9, and H.264 codecs? ▼
AV1 is the newest, highest-quality format but may not play on older devices. VP9 is standard for most high-res web video. H.264 (AVC) is the most widely compatible format that will play on virtually any television, phone, or computer.
Is there a file size limit? ▼
Our backend utilizes multi-threaded downloading with 6 concurrent connections. While there is no hard cap, downloading multi-hour 4K documentaries may take a few minutes to process and merge before the download prompt appears.
Can I download private or age-restricted videos? ▼
Yes, our engine utilizes advanced client spoofing (masking as Android VR and Smart TVs) to bypass standard age gates. However, completely private videos require authorized session cookies which are restricted for public usage.
Why do downloads sometimes hang at "Processing"? ▼
Once the stream reaches 100%, the FFmpeg engine takes over to merge the heavy video and audio tracks. For a 4K video, this localized stitching process can take 10-30 seconds depending on server load. Do not refresh the page.
Do you store copies of the videos I download? ▼
No. We respect strict data privacy protocols. Your file is saved to an isolated temporary sandbox during the merge process and is completely wiped from our servers immediately after your download finishes.
Why are WebM formats excluded from the list? ▼
WebM is an open media container specifically built for web browsers. However, it often fails to play natively on Apple devices (iPhones/Macs) or desktop media players without third-party plugins. We strictly enforce MP4 delivery for maximum compatibility.
Is it legal to use this tool? ▼
This tool is intended strictly for creating personal, offline backups of public domain videos, your own content, or content under creative commons. Users are responsible for adhering to local copyright laws and YouTube's Terms of Service.
